AGRICULTURE & ENVIRONMENT

THE VALUE OF KENYA TEA

Tea is an evergreen shrub that drives Kenya's agricultural economy, with over 585,000 metric tonnes produced annually. Click below to read the complete research article.

Tea is an evergreen shrub or small tree cultivated and processed to make dried leaves for drinking, as well as for medicinal and herbal purposes.

According to Chinese legend, tea was used as early as 2700 BCE as herbal medicine. By the 3rd century, it became a daily beverage where processing and cultivation flourished. By 350 BCE, the first records of planting, processing, and drinking were documented. Tea cultivation continued to spread worldwide, arriving in Kenya in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

Researchers Joizy Pius Engunjobi and Stephen Asatsa highlighted that 95.3% of Kenyans consume tea daily, with 76.4% drinking 2 to 3 cups per day.

The Kenya Tea Industry Performance Report (2024) indicated that Kenya produced approximately 585,533 metric tonnes of processed tea, making it the third-highest producer globally after China and India.

The Tea Board of Kenya categorizes production into two main regional blocks:

  • East of Rift Block (32%): Mt. Kenya region (Kiambu, Murang'a, Nyeri, Kirinyaga, Meru, Tharaka Nithi), Nyambeni Hills, and the Aberdare Ranges.
  • West of Rift Block (68%): Nandi, Trans Nzoia, Elgeyo Marakwet, Kakamega, Vihiga, Kericho, Bomet, Kisii, Nyamira, and Nakuru.

Optimal growing conditions require Kenya's high potential highlands (1,500m–2,700m above sea level) with annual rainfall between 1,200mm–1,500mm. Tea plants (Camellia sinensis) remain in nurseries for 9–12 months before transplanting, taking two years until the first harvest, and can remain productive for 50 to 80 years.

Climate Change Threat: Studies by Eco-Business project that optimal conditions for tea in Kenya could decline by 26.2% by 2050 due to rising temperatures and extreme weather events, presenting a major challenge for the agricultural sector.
